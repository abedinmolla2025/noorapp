### 68 · Al-Qalam (upgraded: PARTIALLY_VERIFIED → A)
- Theme ("reassures the Prophet, highly praises his character, urges steadfastness… parable of the garden") now VERIFIED: The Clear Quran + Maududi's own IIUM-hosted "Chapter Introductions" page (independent of Khattab).

- Draft: *"Surah Al-Qalam ('The Pen') is a Meccan surah of 52 verses. It opens with an oath by the pen, reassures the Prophet, and praises his outstanding character."*

## Class C — detailed (genuine revelation-place disputes; drafts are human-review artifacts)

### 55 · Ar-Rahman
- Name ("The Beneficent") and 78 verses VERIFIED (Quran.com API, Tanzil, QuranEnc, Maulana Muhammad Ali).
- Dispute documented with strong authorities: Maududi (majority Makki + Madani traditions from Ibn Abbas/Ikrimah/Qatadah); Muhammad Asad ("Period Uncertain"; Zamakhshari + Suyuti → Medina; Baydawi open; most commentators → Meccan); Islahi (notes and rejects the Madani view); Abdel Haleem's own surah-55 header ("A Medinan sura"). Repo data + Quran.com API + Tanzil hold the Medinan side.
- Draft: *"Ar-Rahmaan (The Beneficent) is a surah consisting of 78 verses."*

### 60 · Al-Mumtahanah (first clean draft this round)
- Name ("The Woman to be Examined"), 13 verses, name origin (testing emigrant women, v.10), themes (loyalty/Abraham as model; no befriending enemies; courtesy to non-persecutors permitted) — all VERIFIED via The Clear Quran + Enlightening Commentary vol. 18 (independent).
- Dispute: The Clear Quran ("This Medinian sûrah…") + Tanzil (Medinan) vs Enlightening Commentary ("revealed in Mecca").
- Draft: *"Al-Mumtahanah (The Woman to be Examined) has 13 verses and takes its name from the examination of believing emigrant women. It forbids believers from befriending hostile enemies while permitting courtesy toward those who do not fight or expel them, and holds up Prophet Abraham as a model."*

### 76 · Al-Insan

- Dispute: Maududi (majority-Makki classical authorities: Zamakhshari, Razi, Baydawi, Nisaburi, Ibn Kathir; Alusi's majority report) vs Enlightening Commentary (Medinan consensus citing Qurtubi) vs IslamReligion (records both). Alternate name "Hal-Ata" lacked a second acceptable source → excluded.

Its theme is the Hereafter: it warns those who defraud in measure and weight of the coming Day, contrasts the recorded deeds of the wicked with the reward of the righteous, and closes with the believers vindicated over the disbelievers who mocked them."*
