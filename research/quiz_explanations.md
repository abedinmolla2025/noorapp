## 9322e182 — How many Prophets are mentioned by name in the Quran? (key → 25)

The Quran names 25 prophets: 18 are listed together in Surah Al-An'am
(6:83–86), and 7 more are named elsewhere (Adam, Idris, Hud, Shu'ayb, Salih,
Dhul-Kifl, Muhammad ﷺ). No reputable source supports the figure of 20.
Sources: islamqa.info/en/10468; islamqa.org/askmufti 45203.

## 1e8646d1 — How many minarets does Masjid al-Haram currently have? (key → 13)

Masjid al-Haram's minaret count grew across the Saudi expansion eras
(7 → 9 → 11 → 13); it currently has 13. The question says "currently"
because an unqualified count is time-ambiguous.
Sources: una-oic.org (2025); saudipedia.com Grand Mosque minarets.

## cc209308 — Who is the true mujahid? (rebuilt on Tirmidhi 1621)

The "greatest vs lesser jihad" report is graded weak and not authentically
Prophetic (Permanent Committee, al-Bayhaqi, Ibn Hajar). The sound hadith is
Tirmidhi 1621: the true mujahid is the one who strives against his own self
(nafs) in obedience to Allah — graded Hasan Sahih by at-Tirmidhi,
authenticated by al-Hakim and al-Albani (as-Sahihah 549).
Sources: sunnah.com/tirmidhi:1621; al-feqh.com fatwa compilation.

## f4f1cb3c — Sajdah tilawah count (Hanafi, key 14 retained)

The Hanafi school counts 14 verses of prostration (sajdah tilawah) in the
Quran. Other counts (e.g., 15) come from different scholarly positions, so
the question names the Hanafi school to remove the ambiguity.
Sources: Sajdah-Tilawat research paper; HRMARS sujud-tilawah study.

## bf41f99d — Sajdah tilawah count (Ibn Baz / Permanent Committee, key 15 retained)

Ibn Baz and the Permanent Committee count 15 verses of prostration. The
question names this authority because sajdah counts differ between scholarly
positions — and 15 must not be misattributed to the Shafi'i/Hanbali schools.
Sources: abdurrahman.org sujud-ul-tilawah; islamqa.info 5126.
